# Iconic requisitions and Apogee — 0.18.0

## References and adaptation

Read the [Gamestegy Apogee entry](https://gamestegy.com/cyberpunk-2077/wiki/3049/militech-apogee-sandevistan) and [cyberware catalogue](https://gamestegy.com/cyberpunk-2077/wiki/cyberware). These identify the Militech “Apogee” as a Tier 5 Iconic, 44 capacity, 85% slowing, toggleable charge, critical/headshot bonuses, kill-based duration extension and stamina recovery. The entry displays the upgraded profile at 6 seconds, 25-second cooldown, +20% headshot/critical chance/critical damage, +10% duration per kill and +22% stamina. Cybernetics 2077 uses tiers 1–5 rather than 5+/5++; its first Apogee uses those displayed values as one Tier 5 model.

Fandom's page and API returned HTTP 402, and the Fextralife endpoint failed. Those endpoints were not used as evidence. An initially guessed Gamestegy numeric ID resolved to unrelated equipment; the actual Apogee URL was obtained from its cyberware catalogue. No downloaded reference page or game artwork is redistributed.

Minecraft's 32-block encounter clock keeps the active user's movement/weapon recovery at 1.25× and ordinary affected simulation at 0.1875×: 85% slower relative to the user. Active Dynalar users in an Apogee field have 0.375× pace. Equal Apogee users remain equal; overlapping fields take the strongest applicable source once. Existing PvP/team exclusions, native world/sun clocks and first-person trail safeguards remain.

Minecraft has no stamina resource. The kill bonus removes 0.88 food exhaustion (22% of one four-point exhaustion cycle), bounded at zero; it never creates food or saturation. Enemy deaths extend the current battery by 12 ticks, capped at 120 ticks remaining. Passive animals do not grant it. Native arrow/trident impact coordinates identify the upper quarter of a living hitbox for the headshot bonus. A target-UUID and same-tick marker is consumed on the matching damage call; it cannot carry over to another target or later hit. Existing weapon critical probability composition and its 50% cap remain.

The last battery model is persisted separately from the installed tier. This preserves Dynalar save compatibility and retains the correct recharge denominator across model changes/removal. Reinstallation does not mint a full charge. Apogee can start with enough charge for one native tick; the existing one-second rearm still applies. Model identity and battery profile are included in existing state snapshots for the client field, HUD and remote trails.

## Acquisition and UI

Every chromed mob keeps its guaranteed damaged-part drop. An independent bonus roll can add one requisition chip: `0.01 × averageTier + 0.005 × (implantCount − 1)`, from 1% to 7.5%. Chip tier is an independent roll through the existing loadout-based salvage distribution. The same mob-loot rule and one-death ledger guard both drops. Chips are tiered 1–5 to support future Iconics; this release only sells a Tier 5 Iconic. No conversion recipe or automatic lower-tier substitution exists.

Apogee appends after all existing catalogue entries, including elemental Mantis variants. The daily assortment algorithm, starter-optics guarantee, tier weighting and ten-offer limit remain. It becomes eligible at Street Cred 40 and costs €$75,000 plus one Tier 5 requisition chip, three times an ordinary Tier 5 implant's €$25,000 base price. A chip neither stocks the doctor nor grants an implant directly.

The server checks NPC proximity/aliveness, menu tab/carried stack, reputation, current stock, exact chip tier, money and inventory room before purchase. All purchase packet paths enforce Iconic payment; the salvage path cannot discount it. A singleton payment can free its own inventory slot. Delivery failures restore money and the consumed payment. The client uses the existing menu, gold Iconic row accents, an ACQUIRE button and a tooltip listing chip count and full cost. The equipment detail page also names the Iconic classification. Pixel-art chips and the shielded spine are original 32×32 textures rendered through ordinary cached item models; no new world renderer, particles or per-tick loot scans are added.

## Validation scope

Five added JVM cases cover monotonic/bounded chip odds, invalid loadouts, model-specific battery/critical values, unequal/equal-user ratios and unchanged Dynalar profiles. Eight added dedicated-server cases exercise actual payment/delivery, wrong-tier and salvage rejection, inventory/funds/stale-stock/reputation failures, controlled rare drops and duplicate/mob-loot protection, model interaction/PvP, persisted and legacy battery states, real enemy deaths and passive-animal exclusion, headshot event/damage matching, installed capacity/socket and client snapshots.

The native client review buys from the real stock menu using its button, confirms chip and eddie consumption, installs through the actual installation menu, inspects the detail page, activates with O, checks local client bubble strength, draws own full-body echoes in first person, and deactivates into the recharge HUD. Native captures use a disposable integrated world at 1280×720 with software OpenGL. They do not establish hardware performance, audio or authenticated two-computer multiplayer. The headshot fixture supplies native impact events at chosen heights and invokes actual damage; it does not claim an end-to-end projectile trajectory at every target shape.

Existing catalogue-size assertions were updated for the appended item. A pre-existing scheduler persistence fixture asserted the entire dimension had no metadata after clearing its own position; earlier batches can legitimately leave other positions. It now checks that the cleared position has no metadata, while retaining the timer-restoration and actual delay assertions. Initial new-test failures from a negative reputation adjustment and a long-to-int payment call were fixture issues; the production account correctly rejects negative reputation. The final fixtures restore account snapshots to test lower reputation.
